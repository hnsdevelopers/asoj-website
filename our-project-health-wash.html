Health & WASH Programme

Promoting hygiene, health and well-being for families in Ghazipur and Gharoli.

Programme overview

The Health & WASH (Water, Sanitation and Hygiene) programme addresses critical health challenges faced by marginalised communities in Ghazipur and Gharoli. Through health camps, vaccination drives and hygiene-awareness sessions, it has measurably improved community health outcomes.

The Mahila Panchayat team played a key role in COVID-19 relief and continues to organise health interventions for women, children and elderly community members.

Health camps & medical support

Multiple health camps organised through the Mahila Panchayat network have benefited women and children from Ghazipur slum and Sapera colony, providing medical services, medicines and health guidance to families who cannot afford regular healthcare.

COVID-19 response highlights

  • Distribution of thermal scanners, oximeters, masks and sanitisers
  • Support provided to Anganwadi workers, ASHA workers and medical officers
  • PPE kits, masks, gloves and sanitisers supplied to Lal Bahadur Shastri Hospital
  • 2 vaccination camps organised in the Gharoli area with CRY's support

Vaccination awareness campaigns

A vaccination-awareness campaign was carried out through household contacts, nukkad meetings, street plays and puppet shows, and poster/pamphlet distribution — reaching over 10,000 people. As a result, 90% of the adult population in target areas was vaccinated.

Document support camps

Recognising that many women lacked essential identification documents, ASOJ organised special camps:

Aadhaar & PAN card camp

270 people obtained Aadhaar and PAN cards

Health card camp

40 people benefited from the health-card facility

Abha health cards

300 families received Abha digital health-ID cards

Bank account camp

45 women opened accounts with Bank of Baroda

Menstrual health & hygiene

Regular sessions address stigma and promote safe practices. Most girls and adolescents were initially hesitant to discuss menstruation, but through repeated sessions have learned to speak openly.

Menstruation Day celebration

70 women and 40 adolescent girls took part in awareness activities and received sanitary pads.

Monthly pad distribution

Sanitary pads distributed monthly by the PINKISHE Foundation to adolescent girls and women.

Cervical-cancer awareness

Sessions on the menstrual cycle and cervical-cancer prevention.

22 meetings held

125 children (32 boys, 93 girls) took part in menstrual-hygiene sessions.
