About Mahila Panchayat

Mahila Panchayat is a community-based initiative, run with support from the Delhi Commission for Women, giving women a platform to raise grievances and seek justice at the community level. It resolves domestic violence, family disputes and other concerns through collective, doorstep dialogue rather than the courts.

Objectives

  • Provide an accessible justice mechanism for women
  • Address domestic violence and family disputes
  • Empower women through collective decision-making
  • Build awareness of women's legal rights

Key achievements

  • Mediated a large number of family disputes successfully
  • Built confidence in women to speak up
  • Strengthened community support systems
  • Secured legal and medical support for survivors

Our approach

Through Mahila Panchayat, we provide a safe space for women to discuss their problems and find collective solutions. Trained women from the community act as mediators and counsellors, resolving issues through dialogue and mutual understanding — with formal hearings, home visits, and (where needed) referrals to police or de-addiction services.

Supported by Delhi Commission for Women Supported by Azim Premji Foundation

Recent activities

A sample from our activity register for August 2025–July 2026, which logs 17 programmes in total. These eight are documented in full detail in our records.

  • 27 August 2025

    Legal Awareness Camp — Domestic Violence Act, 2005

    57 women attended a session at the ASOJ office led by Advocate Monika ji of Saket Court, who explained the different forms domestic violence can take — verbal, mental, physical, economic and sexual — and covered women's property rights, followed by an open Q&A.

  • 9 September 2025

    Gender Sensitisation Workshop

    Around 40–50 women gathered at a venue in Mayur Vihar Phase-3 for a session that opened with an icebreaker, moved into a discussion on how household duties are divided by gender, included a screening of the short film "Juice," and covered workplace sexual-harassment law before closing with group discussion and dance.

  • 22–25 September 2025

    "Chuppi Todo" (Break the Silence) Campaign

    A four-day campaign against domestic violence at the Mahila Panchayat office in Sapera Basti, with around 40–50 women each day: art and slogan-writing on day one, an original feminist song and street-play rehearsal on day two, personal reflective writing on day three, and a community rally with a street performance on day four, reaching women not yet connected to the Mahila Panchayat.

  • 27 October 2025

    Capacity-Building Training — Dispute Resolution & Counselling

    22 Mahila Panchayat members were trained at Ambedkar Bhawan by facilitators from Action India. The session used personal storytelling, a comparison of how a traditional (male-led) panchayat and a Mahila Panchayat each handle a case, a walkthrough of court procedure, and a role-play covering the full arc of a case — hearing, safety planning, mediation and follow-up.

  • January 2026

    Monthly Activities

    A month of sessions across age groups on menstrual hygiene, child marriage, gender discrimination, POCSO, domestic violence and women's rights, plus a nukad (street-corner) meeting in Ghazipur reaching 110 women. Two individual outcomes stood out: a 16-year-old named Anshu returned home after a peer's intervention, and a girl named Jahanur re-enrolled in class 10 and resisted a child marriage.

  • 2 February 2026

    Legal Awareness Camp — POCSO Act

    63 participants (women and adolescents aged 14–18) learned about the POCSO Act, how to recognise inappropriate touch, personal boundaries, and how to reach the Child Helpline (1098) and the women's helpline.

  • February 2026

    Monthly Activities — Door-to-Door Outreach

    1,007 households were reached door-to-door across Ghazipur and Sapera Basti, alongside nukad meetings on POCSO, gender, menstruation, child marriage and workplace harassment (POSH), and adolescent-group sessions on child trafficking and sexual-exploitation awareness.

  • 23–28 February 2026

    3rd Awareness Campaign — Gender Empowerment

    A five-day campaign spanning Mulla Colony and Ghazipur Dairy Farm: day one covered the economic value of unpaid household work (estimated at over ₹13,000 a month); day two centred on the impact of domestic violence on families, drawing on women's own experiences; days three to five combined three street plays — on dowry, domestic violence, and unequal education for sons and daughters — with a community rally carrying the message, "We will not tolerate domestic violence."
